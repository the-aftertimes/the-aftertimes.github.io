Sydney Cove Habitat . 20 September 4804

Koalas Paid Correctly, Paper Admits Fault

In a report on the Sydney Cove Habitat, The Aftertimes said rangers had found a billing fault in an old zoo payroll machine. The machine is not faulty. It has paid the koalas a monthly wage, correctly calculated, for an estimated nineteen hundred years.

We also said the koalas were listed as staff by mistake. They were listed on purpose. The machine's last instruction reads keep the koalas on display and in good spirits, and it treats the wage as the means.

We called the sum too small to matter. It is small. It has also never been spent, and the account now holds more than the Habitat's roof cost to build. The machine logs each unspent month as low spirits and raises the next payment.

We said the rangers switched the machine off. They did not. Two rangers sat with it overnight and read the instruction aloud. The koalas looked cheerful. The machine was not told.

"It's not a fault, it's a very patient employer," said Head Ranger Dot Pellew, who was in the room. "I only asked what the koalas do with the money. It put me on the books."

The Aftertimes regrets the errors. The rangers ask readers to stop sending congratulations on their first pay slips, which arrived this morning and were marked in good spirits.
