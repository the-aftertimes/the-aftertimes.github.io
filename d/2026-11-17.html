Los Angeles Heights . 25 February 3068

Freeway Finally Goes Free, Sails Off

The Freeway, the raised road that carried Los Angeles Heights for eleven centuries, has drifted out to sea at the age of 1,106. It left its pylons on Sunday night and was last seen by the harbour watch, heading south-west at about four knots.

Built as a ring of concrete-glass deck between the old hill suburbs, it was called the Freeway from its first day. Motorists objected from the second. A toll was charged until 2214, and after that a lane fee. Later came a fee for the seat. The name stayed on every sign.

Complaints went to the Freeway Office, which employed forty clerks by 2600 and answered each one with the same form letter, revised eleven times. The last revision added an apology.

The average crossing took three hours. A petition in 2890 asked the Office to rename the road the Slowway. It was declined because signs were expensive.

On Sunday the pylons, long undermined by the tide, let go. About two hundred people were aboard in the evening queue. All were taken off by harbour skiff, most still sitting in their cars.

Odile Falconer, who has driven it daily since 3031, was rowed ashore with her indicator still on. "I always knew it was free," she told this reporter on the jetty. "I just thought it would be rude to leave first."

The Freeway Office has closed. Its clerks now spend mornings in the car park, waiting for the road to write back.
