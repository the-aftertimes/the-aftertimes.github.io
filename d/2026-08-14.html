Solder-Bays, Generation Freighter Argus . 2 April 2753

Worker Leans Out, Becomes Foreign Cargo

A two-year spatial survey aboard the generation freighter Argus has established that human rights extend precisely fifteen centimetres past the outer hull plating.

The finding resolved a decade of disputes over tool salvage rights. However, it created an immediate administrative obstacle during routine maintenance on Thursday morning.

Senior hull-sweeper Prahlad Nayar leaned twenty centimetres past the threshold while scraping lichen off a sensor dish. Under fleet law, his upper torso ceased to be a recognised person. When his magnetic boots slipped, his entire body crossed into unmapped void, revoking his citizenship instantly.

Because Nayar is no longer a citizen, pulling his tether back inside the ship would constitute importing unmanifested biological cargo without a clearance manifest.

"The geometry is immaculate," said lead juris-cartographer Nnamdi Eze. "Inside the line, you have human rights. Outside the line, you are raw material. If we reel him in now, we are smuggling."

Nayar remains attached to the hull by a safety line while officers draft an emergency import declaration. Ship authorities cannot supply his suit with extra oxygen, as sending pressurised gas across the border requires a separate export licence.

He remains outside.

Earlier today, a crewmate pushed a nutrient paste tube across the boundary using a fiberglass pipe. Nayar ate it.

Customs inspectors have opened an inquiry into whether the tube was legally dumped.
