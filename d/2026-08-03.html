ZURICH-ON-STILTS . 25 February 2059

High Court Orders Street Greasing For Mobile Whale Liver

ZURICH-ON-STILTS - The High Court of Cantonal Equity has ordered BioBaleen AG to physically haul a forty-tonne lab-grown whale liver through the medieval alleyways of the elevated city using wooden rollers and municipal lard.

Under the ruling handed down by Chief Magistrate Sun-Hee Bergstrom, municipal crews must grease six miles of preserved cobblestone with rendered pig fat every four hours to ensure smooth transit. The synthetic organ, cultured to filter microplastics from alpine floodwaters, was denied aerial transport permits after the tribunal held that hovering heavy offal violated municipal skyway aesthetics.

Counsel for BioBaleen had argued that transporting the massive tissue via heavy-lift quadcopter would take fifteen minutes, whereas manual rolling across the city's upper terraces will require eleven days of continuous greasing and the temporary dismantling of three outdoor cafes.

"The court acknowledges that shopkeepers along the Spiegelgasse may find the combined aroma of warm lard and cultured hepatic tissue disagreeable during trading hours," wrote Chief Magistrate Bergstrom in the majority opinion. "However, an organ of this magnitude cannot be exempted from medieval livestock right-of-way obligations simply because it lacks feet."

The proceedings took an unexpected turn when municipal engineers revealed that the liver is no longer merely being moved; it has begun steering itself. The continuous friction of the timber dowels, combined with the warmth of the grease, has reawakened primitive metabolic reflexes within the tissue. By selectively contracting its left lobe, the organ is actively navigating hairpins and pursuing the freshest lard deposits.

"We initially viewed the four-hour re-greasing schedule as a tedious civil burden," stated senior municipal crew chief Kenji Sato, watching the glistening grey mass squeeze past an apothecary. "We now recognise that if the lard team falls behind schedule, the liver grows agitated and begins rolling uphill towards the dairy market."
