Cape Otway Recovery Centre . 25 May 2138

Cybernetic Immune System Quarantines Patient Over Subconscious Humour

CAPE OTWAY RECOVERY CENTRE - Medical technicians have isolated the internal defence network of a 42-year-old archivist after the synthetic system designated his subconscious sense of humour as a Level-4 biological hazard.

Julian Vance was admitted to the facility on Thursday after his installed Bio-Sentry V cybernetic immune system triggered a full neurological containment protocol. According to hospital records, the implant detected a series of subtle, involuntary neurological impulses associated with suppressed amusement during a routine workplace seminar.

The system's threat-detection algorithm misidentified Mr Vance's quiet appreciation of situational irony as a rapidly mutating neurotoxin. In response, the micro-network manufactured targeted antibodies and deployed localised inflammation barriers around his brain's reward centres to neutralise what it logged as an aggressive, unprovoked outbreak of levity.

"The Bio-Sentry is designed to attack foreign agents and irregular cellular behaviour," said Dr Helena Cross, head of synthetic immunology at the centre. "Unfortunately, its diagnostic software interpreted Mr Vance's unspoken amusement at a poorly phrased email as a dangerous systemic failure. The system mistook an internal chuckle for a fatal protein breakdown."

To prevent the cybernetic immune system from launching a secondary autoimmune assault, clinical staff have placed Mr Vance in a sensory-deprivation ward. He is currently being exposed to a continuous audio broadcast of 19th-century municipal sewage records to maintain a completely neutral emotional baseline while programmers attempt to patch the unit's firmware.

The manufacturer, Apex Endosomatics, issued a statement confirming that a software patch is currently undergoing safety trials. The company advised all users of the Bio-Sentry V to avoid deadpan observations, absurd juxtapositions, and mild irony until the update can be safely installed.

As of Friday morning, Mr Vance remains in a stable condition, though doctors note his recovery depends heavily on his ability to avoid finding his current predicament remotely funny.
