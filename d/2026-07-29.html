Product Review: The Symphony-2 Marital Implant Fixes Arguments But Deploys National Guard

When NeuroSomatic Systems released the Symphony-2 emotional-sync marital implant last month, the headline promise was absolute domestic efficiency. By hardwiring two married nervous systems into a real-time bio-feedback loop, the device claims to eliminate emotional lag and render miscommunication obsolete.

In field testing across households in Gisborne Ridge, the consumer benefits were undeniably impressive. Dispute resolution times plummeted by 40 percent. Husbands registered their wives' unspoken irritation before a dishwasher was even incorrectly loaded. Wives instantly perceived the exact, pathetic nature of their partners' social anxiety, bypassing hours of passive-aggressive sulking in favour of immediate, efficient pity.

The trouble lies in the device's high-stress thresholds. When both spouses experience a simultaneous surge of terror, the Symphony-2's cross-coupled algorithm misinterprets the combined biological output as an imminent hostile invasion.

During our test week with volunteers Malcolm and Clara Finch, a minor dispute regarding forgotten anniversary reservations created a rapid neuro-feedback loop of compounding dread. Within two minutes, the implant had auto-dialled emergency services, locked down three suburban residential streets, and prompted the regional defence force to dispatch an armoured response unit to their driveway.

The local council confirmed that sixteen married couples have accidentally triggered red-level civil defence warnings this month alone, briefly overwhelming municipal emergency response teams with reports of terrorised spouses hiding under dining tables.

NeuroSomatic has promised a firmware update by November, noting that while accidental martial law is inconvenient, neither the Finches nor their neighbours could recall what the original argument was about, technically marking a successful resolution.

Verdict: A triumph of modern biomechatronics for calm households, though users are advised to keep emergency civil defence contact cards laminated on the fridge.
