Alpine Snowpack Dies At 4.2 Million Following Heating Dispute

INNICHEN VALLEY, TYROL - The Alpine Snowpack, a natural ice sheet and hydrological authority aged 4.2 million years, passed away quietly on Thursday following a prolonged battle with synthetic geothermal heating contracts. It is survived by three artificial indoor slopes and a municipal slush reserve in Zurich.

Born in the early Pliocene epoch, the snowpack enjoyed a remarkably stable career managing Western Europe's freshwater systems, continental weather patterns, and winter recreation. Colleagues and climatologists described its early millennia as quiet and methodical, characterised by steady glacial creep and reliable spring run-offs.

Trouble began in 2241, when the European Energy Board reclassified sub-alpine bedrock as a high-density heat-sink, leasing subterranean thermal extraction rights to municipal utility consortiums. Under the terms of the contracts, subterranean power stations were legally permitted to vent industrial exhaust upward, provided they reimbursed local tourism boards for lost ski lift revenue.

The snowpack spent its remaining decades waging a futile legal campaign against the thermal leases. Prosecutors arguing on its behalf insisted that five thousand gigawatts of upward floor-warming constituted constructive eviction. However, regional courts repeatedly ruled that natural precipitation lacked standing to contest land-use permits issued to sub-surface radiator networks.

"The snowpack maintained incredible dignity until the end," said Dr Greta Vance, chief curator at the High Alps Preservation Trust. "Even after losing its lower valley glaciers to subterranean spa complexes, it attempted to remain useful by forming light morning frost on high-altitude solar arrays. Unfortunately, the court-mandated ambient heat was simply too binding."

In its final days, the snowpack was reduced to a forty-metre strip of damp shale beneath a shaded canopy near Mont Blanc. A private memorial service will be held on the dry gravel bed of Lake Geneva. In lieu of flowers, citizens are asked to briefly dial down their underfloor thermostats.
