The Obsidian Citadel, Luna . 21 May 3014

Court Rules Sentient Nebula Cannot Copyright Its Own Light

The High Court of Luna has dismissed a landmark copyright suit brought on behalf of a sentient interstellar gas cloud, ruling that cosmic radiation cannot be protected under intellectual property law.

The action was brought by trustees representing the Ophiuchus Drift, an autonomous ionised nebula that has spent the past four centuries orchestrating a slow-motion installation of violet and sodium flares across the Southern Celestial Hemisphere. The trustees argued that the cloud’s deliberate manipulation of thermal emissions constituted an original work of sculpture, and sought licensing fees from deep-space cruise operators offering commercial tours of the display.

In a unanimous judgment delivered from the bench, Chief Justice Vane rejected the claim, citing fundamental boundaries within lunar contract law.

"While this court acknowledges the aesthetic ambition of the appellant’s four-hundred-year flare, an artistic work requires a legally recognised creator," Chief Justice Vane wrote. "A collection of self-aware plasma remains, in the eyes of the law, an atmospheric event. Photons possess no legal standing to execute a work-for-hire contract, nor can electromagnetic radiation be served with a summons."

The court added that granting intellectual property rights to celestial phenomena would set an unworkable precedent, potentially allowing solar flares to demand royalties on planetary auroras, or black holes to sue galleries for unauthorised light absorption.

Counsel for the cloud expressed disappointment, noting that their client had delayed a scheduled supernova to allow the trial to conclude. Following the decision, the Ophiuchus Drift reportedly expressed its displeasure by shifting its spectrum three shades toward infrared, an artistic gesture that legal experts confirmed was entirely unprotected by law and could be freely copied by any nearby space dust.
