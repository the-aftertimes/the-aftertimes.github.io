Halcyon Drift . 16 September 2447

Both Doctors Say 'Probably Nothing'; Patient Gets Two Lives

Makena Nakashima and Isaura Balogun both told the same patient it was probably nothing on Friday, ending the final round of the Halcyon Drift Diagnostics Open in a dead heat.

The patient, a volunteer with a persistent cough, was the last case in the 'Unknown Presentation' round. Nakashima examined her first and gave her verdict at 14:10. Under the Prognosis Act, that phrase fixes a patient's remaining life at eleven years. Balogun used the same words at 14:12.

Balogun said the eleven years should run from the second announcement. Nakashima said the first. Neither would concede the two minutes.

The referee, a certified timekeeper, confirmed both verdicts were binding and that a patient could not have two lengths of life. He said the rules for this were in section nine. Section nine covers equipment failure.

Sudden death began at 15:40 with a second volunteer. Both again said it was probably nothing. Both again named the same figure.

The crowd of roughly nine hundred left at the fourth tie. The patients were walked out by the same door as the spectators.

In the mixed zone, Balogun was asked about a shared title. "I have never shared anything," she said, "and I don't see why a cough should change that."

The patient has gone home with eleven years. She has asked the organisers to say when to start counting, and has been told to ring back after the trophy is sorted.
